When it goes wrong at 3am

3 min read Some experience helps
💡
In plain English
At 3am the instinct is to start rewriting the prompt, because that feels like the part you control. It is almost never the cause, it cannot be verified quickly, and a prompt changed under pressure with no eval run tends to become tomorrow’s incident. Work the list in order instead.

An AI incident is usually one of five things. Working through them in this order finds it quickly, because it goes from cheapest-to-check to hardest.

txtThe triage order
1. IS THE PROVIDER UP?
   Check their status page first, every time. Half of all AI
   incidents are somebody else's incident. Your fallback should
   already have engaged — check whether it did.

2. DID WE CHANGE SOMETHING?
   Deploys, prompt versions, model ids, feature flags, config,
   in the last 24 hours. Look at prompt_ver in the logs: if the
   error rate steps up exactly when a version changed, you are done.

3. DID THE INPUT CHANGE?
   A new customer with unusual documents. A marketing campaign
   bringing a different kind of question. An integration sending
   ten times the volume. Nothing broke — the load changed shape.

4. DID THE DATA CHANGE?
   For RAG: did an indexing job fail, half-complete, or index
   the wrong version of a document? Look at the chunk_ids on bad
   answers and read what those chunks actually say. This is where
   the "confidently wrong about the refund policy" bug lives.

5. IS IT THE MODEL?
   Last, not first. It is the hardest to verify and the least
   likely. Compare against a pinned known-good version on the
   same inputs before concluding anything.
i
The order is deliberate. The instinct at 3am is to start rewriting the prompt, because that is the part you feel you control. It is almost never the cause, it cannot be tested quickly, and a prompt change made under pressure with no eval run tends to become tomorrow's incident.

What to have ready before you need it

A kill switch per feature

Turns the AI path off and falls back to whatever the product did before it existed. Tested. Not tested on the night.

A one-command rollback

To a previous revision and a previous prompt version, independently. They break independently, so they must revert independently.

A saved query for bad answers

“Show me the last fifty calls for this feature with their chunk ids and outputs.” Writing that query while the incident is live wastes twenty minutes.

A written fallback decision

What the product does when the model is unavailable, agreed with someone outside engineering. Decided in a meeting, not by whoever is on call.

✎
Try this
Run the drill. Turn the kill switch on in staging and watch what the user actually sees. Then roll a prompt version back and time it. Both should take under a minute and neither should require reading documentation. If either surprises you, you have found the work that is worth doing this week rather than at 3am.
✓ Worth remembering
  • AI systems fail with a 200 OK. Your existing monitoring will not notice.
  • One structured log line per call, and feature, prompt_ver and chunk_ids are the three fields you will wish you had.
  • Trace the whole chain. The slow part is usually not the model.
  • Prompts are code: versioned, reviewed, pinned models, evals gating the merge.
  • Shadow, then canary, then widen. Keep a kill switch.
  • Four operational panels plus one quality signal, and alert on cost.
  • Triage in order: provider, our change, input, data, model. The model is last.
The assistant started giving wrong answers this morning. Nobody deployed anything. Where do you look?Answer

At the data and at the inputs, not at the model. For a retrieval system: did an indexing job fail or half-complete, and are the passages being retrieved actually the current ones? Read what those chunks say — it is very often a superseded document that never got removed.

The other common answer is that the traffic changed shape rather than the system: a new customer, a campaign, an integration sending a kind of question you never had before. Nothing broke; the load is different.
