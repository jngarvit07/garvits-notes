Rolling out a change without holding your breath

2 min read Some experience helps
💡
In plain English
You cannot fully test quality before shipping, so the way you ship has to absorb some of that risk. Run the new version alongside the old on real traffic without showing it to anyone; then to a small percentage; then widen. Each step buys evidence that no amount of testing beforehand could give you.

Because you cannot fully test quality in advance, the deployment mechanism has to carry some of the load.

TechniqueHow it worksCosts you
Shadow runRun the new version on real traffic in parallel. Log both, show the user the old one.Double the tokens. Zero risk. The best first step, and the most underused.
Canary1% of traffic on the new version. Watch the metrics. Widen if they hold.Nothing much. This should be the default for prompt changes.
A/B test50/50 split, measure a real outcome — resolution rate, thumbs up, escalations.Time. You need enough traffic for the result to mean anything.
Kill switchA flag that reverts to the previous version, or disables the feature entirely.One flag to maintain. Nobody has ever regretted having one.
i
Shadow running deserves more attention than it gets. It is the only technique here that gives you real-traffic evidence at zero user risk, and the questions real users ask are reliably stranger than the ones in your eval set. A day of shadow traffic regularly finds a failure mode that a hundred hand-written cases missed.
