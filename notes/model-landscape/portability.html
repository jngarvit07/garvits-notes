Staying portable

3 min read No experience needed
💡
In plain English
The models change every few months. The way to survive that is not to pick the right one — it is to make the choice cheap to change later. One function in your codebase that every model call passes through turns “switch provider” from a rewrite into a config change, and it costs you about twenty lines of code.

Models get better, prices move, companies change their terms, and the best choice today will not be the best choice in eighteen months. The goal is not to choose correctly forever — it is to make being wrong cheap to fix.

PracticeWhat it buys you
Model ids in config, never inline in code.Switching becomes a deploy, not a rewrite. It also lets you lock a version in production while testing a new one beside it.
Prompts as files, not string literals.They get reviewed and rolled back like code — and prompts genuinely do need re-tuning when you change model.
An evaluation set you actually maintain.The only way to know that a switch actually made things better. Without it you are choosing on gut feeling and marketing blog posts.
A thin wrapper over the SDK call.One single place to change when a request looks different. Do not build a huge layer on top — the APIs are similar enough that a big one costs more than it saves.
Pin the exact model version in production.A newer version is better on average but it is not the same. Move on purpose, after running your tests — not just because it turned up.
!

Do not build too much on top. The temptation is a grand universal interface that supports every provider equally. In practice it settles at whatever the weakest provider can do, and you lose the very features you chose the model for — caching, thinking, structured outputs, the way tools work. A thin joining layer gives you portability. A whole framework gives you a second product to maintain.

✓ Worth remembering
  • Products, models and platforms are three different levels. Most confused comparisons are simply mixing up the levels.
  • Copilot is a brand name across several unrelated products, not a model. Ask which one, and running on what.
  • Who leads the benchmarks keeps changing, and it rarely predicts your result. Thirty real examples beat every leaderboard.
  • Hard limits — where data may live, cloud contracts, purchasing rules — usually settle this before quality gets a say.
  • Start with the smallest model and move up only where it fails. One product can sensibly use three.
  • Model ids in config, prompts in files, test sets kept up to date. Make being wrong cheap to fix, instead of trying to be right forever.
Why is being able to switch models quickly worth more than picking the best one today?Answer

Because today's ranking lasts a few months, while the code you write around it lasts years. A system that can change model in one afternoon gets the benefit of every improvement that comes along. One with provider-specific calls scattered all through it is stuck with a decision made at the moment you knew the least.

It is also your backup plan for outages. A provider having a bad day becomes a config change instead of an incident.
