Advanced RAG

Why most bad RAG answers are retrieval failures, five chunking strategies and the one that usually wins, hybrid search and rank fusion, reranking, transforming the question before you search, permissions as a query filter, and the metrics that make tuning an engineering exercise.

The topics

01 Why basic RAG disappoints Basic RAG works in a demo and disappoints in production. The gap is almost never the model — it is that the right passage was never retrieved. 2 min 02 Chunking: the decision you make once Where you cut a document decides what can ever be retrieved from it. Five strategies, and the one that is right far more often than the default. 2 min 03 Hybrid search: the highest-value upgrade Vector search alone misses exact terms — product codes, error numbers, names. The fix is to run two searches and combine them, and it is the single highest-value upgrade available. 2 min 04 Reranking Retrieve twenty cheaply, then spend real compute deciding which five actually answer the question. The step with the best return for its cost. 2 min 05 Transforming the question first The question a user types is often a poor search query. Rewriting it before searching fixes follow-up questions, vague questions and questions containing several at once. 2 min 06 Permissions and untrusted passages Retrieval is a security boundary. Filter inside the query, never after it — and treat everything that comes back as untrusted text. 2 min 07 Measuring retrieval on its own Retrieval has its own metrics, separate from answer quality. Measuring them is what turns tuning from opinion into engineering. 2 min
