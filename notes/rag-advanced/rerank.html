Reranking

2 min read Read the AI note first
💡
In plain English
A sift then a proper look. Vector search compares a question to a chunk without ever seeing them together — both were turned into numbers separately, long before the question existed. A reranker reads the question and the passage side by side and scores how well one answers the other. Far more accurate, far too slow to run over a million documents, and perfect for the twenty you just narrowed down to.
txtWhy the two stages differ
RETRIEVAL (bi-encoder)
  embed(question)  ....compare....  embed(chunk)     [computed last week]
  The chunk's vector was made with NO knowledge of your question.
  Fast: millions of comparisons per second. Approximate.

RERANKING (cross-encoder)
  score( question + chunk together, in one pass )
  The model reads both at once and judges the actual match.
  Slow: tens to hundreds per second. Accurate.

  So: retrieve 20-50 the fast way, rerank to the best 3-5 the accurate way.
ApproachQualityLatencyNotes
No rerankingBaseline—Fine for small, clean, well-structured corpora
Hosted reranker APILarge improvement~100–300msThe usual starting point. One call, no infrastructure.
Self-hosted cross-encoderSimilar~50–200msA small model on a modest GPU. Cheaper at volume, and keeps data in-house.
An LLM as judgeBest, sometimesSecondsExpensive and slow. Reasonable for offline evaluation, rarely for live traffic.
!
More passages is not better. Sending fifteen chunks instead of five typically makes answers worse: the relevant one gets diluted, the model has more opportunity to blend two sources into a claim neither made, and you pay for all of it. Retrieve widely, then be ruthless about what actually reaches the prompt. Three excellent passages beat fifteen adequate ones.
