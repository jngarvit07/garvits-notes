The frameworks you will hear named

3 min read No experience needed
💡
In plain English
You will hear three names. The EU AI Act is law, and it sorts systems by risk tier. NIST AI RMF is a voluntary framework in the US describing a sensible process. GDPR is not AI-specific but applies the moment you process personal data — which most AI features do. Knowing which one someone means is half of understanding the question.
FrameworkWhat it isApplies when
EU AI ActBinding law with the risk tiers above. Phased in over several years, with real penalties.You place a system on the EU market or its output is used in the EU — regardless of where you are.
NIST AI RMFA voluntary US framework organised around four functions: Govern, Map, Measure, Manage.Useful anywhere as a structure. Often what US customers ask you to align with.
ISO/IEC 42001A certifiable management-system standard for AI — the AI equivalent of ISO 27001.Enterprise procurement starts asking once you sell to large organisations.
GDPR and similarData protection law. Predates the AI rules and applies fully to them.Any personal data anywhere in training, retrieval, prompts or logs. This one probably already applies to you.
Sector rulesFinancial, medical, legal and employment regulators each have their own requirements.Often stricter and more specific than the general AI rules. Check these before the AI-specific ones.
i

This is orientation, not legal advice, and the details move — the EU AI Act in particular phases obligations in over several years. When a real obligation is in play, the question goes to somebody qualified. What you need is enough to recognise which conversation you are in.

GDPR is the one that catches people first

QuestionWhere it bites in an AI system
What is your lawful basis for this processing?Personal data in a retrieval corpus is processing. “It was already in our database” is not a basis for a new purpose.
Can you delete one person’s data on request?Easy in Postgres, harder in a vector index, effectively impossible from a fine-tuned model. Design for this before you fine-tune.
Where is it processed, and by whom?An API call sends data to a provider, possibly across a border. That is a processor relationship needing a contract.
How long do you keep prompts and outputs?Debug logs full of prompts are a personal-data store with no retention policy. This is extremely common.
Is there a solely automated decision with legal effect?If so, people have specific rights — including meaningful human review. A human who rubber-stamps is not review.
!

Your logs are the quiet problem. Teams think hard about the retrieval corpus and then log every prompt and completion to a service with no retention policy, indefinitely. Prompts contain whatever users typed, which is frequently personal and sometimes special-category data. Decide retention deliberately, and redact before writing rather than after.

Your feature summarises support tickets that contain customer names. Which of the three applies?Answer

GDPR, immediately and regardless of the AI angle — you are processing personal data, so you need a lawful basis, a retention period, and an answer for a deletion request that reaches your logs as well as your database.

The EU AI Act probably places this in a low-risk tier with light obligations. The instinct to check the AI-specific law first and the data-protection law second is usually the wrong way round.
