The foundations worth filling in

2 min read No experience needed
💡
In plain English
Some fundamentals keep paying out no matter which framework is fashionable: how HTTP actually works, what a database is doing, how to read a stack trace, git beyond commit and push, and how to write something down so another person can follow it. These age far better than any library.

You can build a great deal without these. They are what separates someone who can wire an API from someone who can debug why the wiring is not working.

TopicWhy it earns its timeDepth needed
PythonThe ecosystem is Python-first. Every paper, notebook, evaluation harness and data tool assumes it, even when your product is TypeScript.Enough to read a notebook and modify it. Not a second career.
Data handlingMost of the real work in an AI system is getting data into a usable shape. Cleaning, joining, sampling, inspecting.Practical fluency. This is the highest-value item on the list.
StatisticsSampling, distributions, confidence. Why thirty cases can mislead you and how to tell when a difference is noise.Enough to not fool yourself with a percentage.
Vectors and similarityWhat an embedding is geometrically, what cosine similarity measures, why nearest-neighbour search needs an index.Intuition, not linear algebra. Half a day.
How transformers workAttention, layers, why context is quadratic and therefore expensive. Explains behaviour rather than enabling it.Conceptual. Watch a good explainer; do not implement one unless you want to.
Distributed systems basicsAI systems are slow, failure-prone network calls. Queues, retries, idempotency, backpressure, timeouts.Working knowledge. This is where production AI actually breaks.
i

You do not need to train a model. The instinct is to think real understanding starts with training, and for the overwhelming majority of applied work it is the least useful thing on this page. Fine-tuning is a specialist tool with a narrow set of cases; almost everything is prompting, retrieval, tools and evaluation.
