What models get wrong, and why

2 min read No experience needed
💡
In plain English
The failures are systematic rather than random, which means they can be designed around. It invents citations because a plausible-looking citation is a good prediction. It is bad at arithmetic because digits are poor material for a next-token guess. It does not know what happened after its training data ended. None of these are fixed by asking it more nicely.
FailureWhy it happensWhat helps
Hallucination — a confident, invented fact, citation or APIPlausible continuation is the only objective. A fake reference is textually excellent.Supply the facts (see RAG), permit "I do not know", and verify anything checkable in code.
Out-of-date answersKnowledge is frozen at the training cut-off.Give current information in the prompt, or a search tool.
Arithmetic and counting slipsIt is predicting text, not calculating.Hand it a calculator or code execution as a tool.
Losing things in a long contextAttention spreads thin over very long inputs; the middle suffers most.Send less: retrieve the relevant part rather than the whole corpus.
Inconsistency between runsSampling is random by design.Lower the temperature, constrain the output shape, and test with real evaluations.
Sycophancy — agreeing with a wrong premiseTuned toward helpfulness, and a confident question is a strong prior.Ask neutrally. Do not smuggle the answer into the question.

Evaluate rather than vibe-check. Twenty to fifty real inputs with known-good answers, run on every prompt change, is the difference between engineering and superstition. Without a test set, every prompt edit is a guess and no regression is ever noticed.

i
A model can grade another model’s output against a rubric, which makes evaluation affordable at volume. It is a useful tool with an obvious limitation: the judge has the same blind spots as the thing it is judging, so keep a human sample.
