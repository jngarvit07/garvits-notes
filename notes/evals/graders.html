How to score an answer

3 min read Some experience helps
💡
In plain English
There are four ways to score an answer, from cheap and strict to expensive and flexible. An exact match, when there is only one right answer. A rule or a pattern, when you can describe what correct looks like. A human, who is the real truth but cannot do thousands. Or another model as the examiner — which works, as long as you have first checked that the examiner agrees with the humans.

Four kinds of marker, cheapest and most reliable first. Use the simplest one that can actually judge your task — and use several together, because most tasks have one part you can check exactly and one part that needs judgement.

GraderHow it worksGood forWatch out
Exact / structuralA text match, a JSON schema check, a pattern.Sorting, pulling out fields, routing, anything with one correct answer.Nothing — use it wherever you can. It costs nothing and it never lies.
ProgrammaticCode that checks qualities: does it parse, does it cite sources, is every cited id a real one, is it within the length limit.Whether it stuck to the sources, whether it followed the format, safety rules, and anything you can write as a simple check.It only checks the things you remembered to check.
LLM-as-judgeA model marks the answer against a written marking scheme.Tone, helpfulness, quality of the reasoning — the parts that really are a matter of judgement.It has its own biases, and it varies more than people expect. See below.
HumanA person reads it and gives it a mark.Checking that the other three are right. This is the real truth that all the others are trying to copy.Slow and expensive — so spend it on checking the other markers, not on doing thousands of cases.
!

Reach for the model examiner last, not first. “Just get a model to mark it” feels like the answer to everything, so it ends up being used for things a three-line check would settle exactly. If the requirement is “returns valid JSON with these five fields”, that is JSON.parse and a schema, not a matter of opinion.

Making an LLM judge trustworthy

Write a real rubric

“Rate helpfulness out of 10” just gives you noise. Write down what each score means, in full sentences, with an example showing the line between one score and the next.

Prefer binary questions

“Is every fact in this answer supported by the given text? Yes or no.” is far steadier than any out-of-10 scale, and far easier to act on.

Show the criteria, not the answer key

If you show it the model answer, the examiner starts rewarding answers that look similar to it rather than answers that are correct. Different wording is not a mistake.

Calibrate against humans

Mark fifty cases by hand and check whether the examiner agrees with you. If it does not, fix the marking scheme before you trust a single score it gives.

i

Model examiners have known biases, and all of them can be measured: they prefer longer answers, they prefer answers written in their own style, and when comparing two options the order you show them in changes which one wins. Shuffle the order and check whether the result still holds.
