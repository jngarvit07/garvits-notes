A worked architecture, end to end

2 min read Some experience helps
💡
In plain English
One system designed all the way down, with the reasoning next to each decision and an explicit list of what is deliberately left out. The omissions are the more useful half — knowing what you are not building, and why, is what keeps a first version shippable.
Real world“Ask a question about your own documents” — the full design

A team uploads their documents and asks questions about them in natural language. Answers must cite their sources. This is the most common AI product shape there is, and it is worth designing all the way down once.

Every decision, and why

DecisionChoiceWhy this one
ShapeB, RAG — not an agentThe steps are known in advance. There is nothing for a model to plan.
Chunk size~800 tokens, 100 overlapBig enough to hold a whole argument, small enough that five fit in context. Overlap stops a sentence being cut in half at a boundary.
Permission filterApplied in the search queryFiltering after retrieval means the forbidden chunk was already in memory and one bug away from the prompt. This is the single most commonly skipped line in RAG systems.
Rerank20 → 5Vector similarity is cheap and approximate; a reranker is expensive and accurate. Use each where it is good.
StreamingYes, to a humanPerceived latency. The citations are sent as structured data alongside, not parsed out of the stream.
HistoryLast 3 turns onlyBounded cost, bounded latency, and it stops an old turn poisoning a new answer.
ModelBehind complete()So the choice stays a configuration value. A cheaper model handles query rewriting; the capable one writes the answer.
What is loggedWhich chunks were usedWithout this you cannot tell a retrieval failure from a generation failure, which is 80% of debugging this system.

What is deliberately not in version one

No agent loop. No semantic cache. No fine-tuning. No multi-provider fallback. Each of those is a real technique and each is a rung you climb when the rung below has visibly failed — with an eval set to prove it did. Building all of them up front produces a system nobody can debug, whose quality nobody can measure.

✓ Worth remembering
  • Architecture is the decisions that are expensive to reverse. Everything else is code.
  • Five layers: interface, orchestration, context, capability, model. Quality problems live in context; risk lives in capability.
  • Four shapes: single call, RAG, workflow, agent. Climb one rung at a time.
  • One wrapper around every model call turns the provider choice into a configuration value.
  • Label the arrows. An unlabelled box diagram is decoration.
  • Decide what “degraded” looks like before you need it.
